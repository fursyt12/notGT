### What's new in 0.14.0 (beta)

**A beta, for trying the NDI sender's new converter.** Everything else is as in 0.12.7;
the NDI sender is what changed. Its default converter stays WebGL2: the new one runs
only when asked for.

- **A native NDI converter (beta), for testing.** The NDI sender can now turn each frame
  into NDI's format in its own process, in Rust: Chromium's frame is taken on the GPU as
  it is and a Vulkan compute shader writes NDI's 4:2:2 straight into memory NDI reads —
  no copy by the CPU, no extra process. On a Radeon 780M (Linux, 1080p30) it took 32–36
  points less of a CPU core per out than WebGL2 (41.6% against 74.2% for a lower third,
  46.4% against 82.2% full screen) and about 160 MB less memory; 30 minutes at 1080p60
  held 60 fps with right colours and flat memory. Built for Windows x64 and Linux x86-64.
  Before an out goes on air it checks itself against the CPU byte for byte, and if it
  cannot work it says why — in the sender's log and under the out in the dashboard — and
  WebGL2 converts instead.
- **Try it on Windows** — in the package, with NDI Tools installed:
  - **NDI test - native converter.cmd** measures the native converter and WebGL2 on this
    computer (about two minutes) and writes one zip to the Desktop: the GPU, its driver
    and Vulkan, both converters' CPU, memory, frame rate, latency, and whether colours
    and alpha come through right. Nothing personal goes in. Send it back.
  - **notGT - native NDI converter.cmd** starts notGT with the native converter.
- **Steady NDI latency.** Chromium paints at 60 fps every
  16 666 µs, not every 1000/60 ms, and the sender's frame clock used to keep its own
  time: frames waited longer and longer for their turn, up to a whole frame, and every
  seven minutes one was dropped. The clock now follows Chromium's. With the native
  converter at 1080p60, latency held at 26–27 ms for 30 minutes, where it went round
  between 26 and 41 ms before, and the sender's CPU no longer rose and fell with it. The
  WebGL2 and CPU converters keep the same clock.
- **Right colours at every NDI size.** NDI decodes the 4:2:2 the GPU converters send as
  Rec.709 at every size, not BT.601 below 720p and Rec.2020 above 1080p as its
  documentation says (measured from 64×32 to 3840×2160): SD and 4K outs sent with the GPU
  came out with shifted colours. Now Rec.709 everywhere.
- **The GPU where there is a real one.** The sender draws on the GPU by default where
  Chromium has a real one and keeps the frame there; on Linux with no display it starts a
  headless compositor to get one. It never forces Chromium onto WebGPU or Vulkan past
  Chromium's own GPU blocklist — that hung GPUs in testing.
- **Converting on the CPU** (with no GPU) runs in WebAssembly SIMD, about three times
  quicker, and never sends more frames than the out's rate.
